Page 09 — Questions

The things people ask first.

Do I have to be an artist?

No. Your Radical Sabbatical is for anyone with something they want to make or work out — founders, researchers, cooks, coders, teachers, people between chapters. The only requirement is wanting the time badly enough to come and take it.

What counts as a project?

Anything with a next step. A book, a business, a thesis, a record, a garden plan, a career change, a hundred photographs you've never edited. If you can describe what moving it forward looks like, it counts.

Is this an opportunity to work remotely for a week?

While a week of working remotely by the ocean sounds exciting, Your Radical Sabbatical is created for you to fully immerse yourself in the creative process. Give yourself the gift of an intentional pause from everyday work, with dedicated time to explore your creative interests and let your creativity flow.

Seventy percent unstructured time sounds terrifying. Help.

That reaction is extremely common, and usually a sign you need this more than most people do. Three things make it work: the morning workshop gives you a running start, the walk resets you when you stall, and the salon means every day ends with other people.

The open time isn't enforced solitude, either. Some people work alone in their rooms all five days. Others colonise a table and work in companionable silence. Both are correct.

Who else will be there?

Thirty people at most, and a deliberately mixed thirty — different disciplines, different stages, different ages. Mixing fields is the point: a novelist and a furniture maker have far more to say to each other than two novelists do.

Thirty is small enough that you'll know everyone's name by Wednesday, and big enough that you can disappear for an afternoon without anybody organising a search party.

What does it cost, and what's included?

$4,000 for a private room, $3,200 for a shared bedroom — per person, for the five days. Exactly what that covers is set out in full with the application, before anyone is asked for a payment.

Registration is the last of the four steps, not the first. You can express interest and apply without committing to anything.

How do I get to Endicott?

Beverly, Massachusetts, on the North Shore — roughly thirty minutes north of Boston by car. Commuter rail runs from Boston's North Station to Beverly, and Logan Airport is under an hour away. Detailed arrival notes come with registration.

Can I attend part of the program?

The program is designed for you to set aside dedicated time to explore your creative interests. Plan to attend the full program.

Can I come with a friend or a partner?

Yes. Apply separately so we can read you both, and say in your application that you'd like to share a room. You can also request shared occupancy and be matched with someone from the cohort.

What if I don't finish anything?

Then you had five days of real creative time, which is the thing almost nobody gets. Finishing is a lovely side effect, not the fee you pay for the week.

I have access needs or a dietary requirement.

There's a space for it on the application, and we'd far rather hear early than politely late. Tell us what you need and we'll tell you honestly what we can do.

Who is actually running this?

One person — who teaches the morning workshop, holds the salon, and reads every single application personally. The staff pass is clipped to the next page.
