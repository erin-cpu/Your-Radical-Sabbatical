Eight years ago I left a nine-to-five to make things, and I have not stopped since. A TEDx programme. A summit. A journal. A book. Malas. Retreats. If you had asked me whether I had a problem making my own work, I would have laughed at you.

But there was still something missing, and it took me an embarrassingly long time to name it.

## It was never about permission

I had permission. I gave myself permission daily, loudly, in writing. What I did not have was a **container** — a bounded stretch of time, in a place that was not my house, with other people doing the same thing in the next room.

Those are different problems and they need different solutions. Permission is something you decide. A container is something somebody has to build.

> Five days is not very long. It is also plenty.

So I am building it. Five days at Endicott College on the Massachusetts coast, summer 2027, where seventy percent of the week has nothing scheduled in it at all.

## Why seventy percent

Because the alternative is what most retreats do, which is fill your days so completely that you go home needing a rest from your rest.

A workshop in the morning to give you a running start. A walk after lunch, if you want one. A salon at night so that every day ends with other people. Everything in between is yours, and nobody is going to check on you.

That proportion is not a compromise. It is the entire product.

## What I'll be doing that week

Teaching the morning workshop, holding the salon, and walking down to the water most afternoons. Find me there if you want to talk your project through — or if you'd much rather not talk at all.
